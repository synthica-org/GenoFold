GenoFold: A Confidence-Aware, Explainable, Multi-Modal AI Framework for 3D Genome Architecture Reconstruction

Abstract: Three-dimensional genome architecture has a vital role in gene regulation, cell identity, and disease mechanisms, but existing methods remain limited due to single inputs, ambiguous inference abilities, and fragmented toolchains that hinder efficiency and reproducibility. We present GenoFold, a confidence-aware, explainable, multi-modal AI framework that integrates Hi-C contact matrices, next-generation sequencing coverage, and genomic metadata to reconstruct 3D chromatin structure within an end-to-end six-stage pipeline. GenoFold encodes genomic loci as a contact graph and applies a four-layer Graph Attention Network along with a six-layer Transformer encoder to display local interaction patterns and long-range chromosomal dependencies. Three-dimensional coordinates are created by combining a learned neural decoder with distance-geometry optimization, while a Monte Carlo dropout uncertainty module produces per-bin group confidence scores and coordinate error bounds for every predicted position.The explainability component is delivered through Integrated Gradients attribution and attention-weight analysis, surfacing influential loci without post-hoc approximation. The ‘GM12878’ in-situ Hi-C dataset was inputted at 100 kb resolution and processes ‘chromosome 22’ within a single pipeline run. Code and data are released as open-source software via Github. Keywords: 3D genome reconstruction, Hi-C contact maps, graph attention network, multi-modal genomic learning, explainable AI

Introduction: The three-dimensional organization of the genome is inseparable from its function. Chromatin folding determines which enhancers can physically contact their target promoters, dictates the boundaries of topologically associating domains (TADs), and divides the nucleus into transcriptionally active and inactive compartments [1]. Disruptions to this spatial organization mechanism have been directly applied in developmental disorders, oncogenesis, and the dysregulation of immune response genes [2].Therefore, reconstructing the 3D architecture of the genome computationally creates a foundation and aids in understanding how the genome encodes and executes biological programs.

The dominant experimental signal for inferring 3D genome structures is Hi-C, a proximity-ligation sequencing assay that measures contact frequencies between pairs of genomic loci at a genome-wide scale [3]. Computational methods that convert these contact matrices into 3D coordinate models have advanced over the past decade. A distance-based approach was used by 3DMax [4], where contact frequency is treated as a proxy for Euclidean distance optimize coordinates via maximum likelihood. A graph-based approach was used by HiC-GNN [5], where graph neural networks are applied directly to the contact matrix to learn structural representations without explicit distance conversion. More recent methods such as FLAMINGO [6] address data and resolution limitations through multi-scale hierarchical inference systems. Despite this progress, three crucial limitations persist across the field.

First, existing methods are unimodal, They rely exclusively on Hi-C contact data, which disregards complementary information available in next-generation sequencing assays. These include chromatin accessibility signals (DNase-seq, ATAC-seq), histone modification tracks (ChIP-seq), and raw coverage depth. As a result, this highly constrains the structural state of a genome. Second, most methods function as block boxes. They produce 3D coordinate predictions without any accurate measure of reliability, making it impossible for users to distinguish high-confidence structural predictions from poorly-supported ones, especially in noisy regions of the contact matrix. Third, existing pipelines are highly fragmented. Data preprocessing, inference, scoring and structural visualization are all handled by separate tools with incompatible interfaces, creating barriers to overall reproducibility.

To address these limitations, we present GenoFold: a confidence-aware, explainable, multi-modal AI framework for end-to-end 3D genome architecture reconstruction. GenoFold integrates Hi-C contact data, NGS coverage tracks, and genomic metadata of species as co-equal inputs rather than using only Hi-C as the sole source of structural information. It employs a graph attention network and transformer encoder that jointly capture local contact patterns and long-range chromosomal dependencies within a single representation. In the end, it produces confidence scores and coordinate-level uncertainty estimates for every predicted bin, allowing users to understand the reliability of each structural prediction.

This tool can make several contributions to AI-driven biotechnology. First, it introduces a framework for 3D genome reconstruction that brings data preprocessing, structural inference, and interpretability together into one pipeline, rather than treating them as separate steps. Unlike previous methods that handle one specific stage, the proposed approach utilizes multi-agent LLMs to integrate multi-modal genomic data, including NGS reads, Hi-C interaction matrices, and genomic metadata, to improve the reconstruction accuracy and provide a complete understanding of the model of chromatin architecture. It also applies advanced AI architectures, including neural networks, transformer-based models, and autoencoders. These are used to address challenges like long-range dependency, noisy data, and structural interpretability. Together, they make genome reconstruction more efficient and accurate. In the end, the model utilizes AI and confidence scoring mechanisms so users are given a clear understanding of the reliability of predictions and gain a nuanced understanding of biological patterns. Overall, by improving accuracy and usability of 3D genome modeling, this research can create tools that could be used by medical professionals to improve patient treatment outcomes.

GenoFold Framework:

Figure 1 shows the complete six-step pipeline in the format of a flowchart.

  1. Stage 1: Data Preprocessing: Raw Hi-C contact matrices are filtered to remove unswappable bins, which are defined as bins whose row-wise zero fraction exceeds a threshold (default is 0.97, relaxed from conventional values to create space for the natural sparsity of real Hi-C data at 100 kb resolution). The remaining bins are normalized using Iterative Correction and Eigenvector decomposition (ICE) [7], which removes systematic biases that arise from GC content, restriction fragment length, and sequence mappability by iteratively scaling rows and columns to equal marginals. ICE convergence is assessed per-iteration via marginal variance, with a tolerance of 1e-5.

Following ICE normalization, a distance-decay correction is utilized by dividing each off-diagonal by its mean contact frequency across the genome, which isolates compartment and TAD structure from the distance-dependent component of contact frequency. Gaussian smoothing (σ=1.0\sigma = 1.0 σ=1.0) is then applied to reduce noise, followed by a log(1+x)(1 + x) (1+x) transform to compress the dynamic range. NGS coverage tracks are normalized through Reads Per Kilobase per Million mapped reads (RPKM), corrected for GC bias through linear regression of coverage on per-bin GC fraction, and z-score standardized. Genomic metadata, which includes species labels, GC content, mappability scores, and ChromHMM chromatin state annotations [8], are encoded as normalized continuous features and 15-class one-hot vectors respectively.

  1. Stage 2: Graph Representation: Preprocessed data are assembled into a genomic contact graph G=(V,E,X,E)\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}, \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{E}) G=(V,E,X,E) where each valid genomic bin makes up a node vi∈Vv_i \in \mathcal{V} vi​∈V, and each nonzero Hi-C contact above a configurable threshold defines a weighted edge (vi,vj)∈E(v_i, v_j) \in \mathcal{E} (vi​,vj​)∈E. Node features X∈RM×F\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times F} X∈RM×F concatenate four feature sources per bin: RPKM-normalized NGS coverage (1 dimension), reverse-complement-collapsed 4-mer frequency vectors (136 dimensions), normalized metadata features including GC content, mappability, and chromatin state (17 dimensions), and sinusoidal bin position (1 dimension), yielding F=155F = 155 F=155 node features. Edge features E∈RE×2\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{E \times 2} E∈RE×2 encode the log-normalized Hi-C contact weight and log-genomic distance for each contact pair. The assembled graph is stored as a PyTorch Geometric \texttt{Data} object [9] and passed directly to the inference stage.
  2. Stage 3: Inference Stage: The inference stage consists of two sequential components:

GNN Encoder. Node features are first projected to 8d8d 8d dimensions via a two-layer MLP with LayerNorm and SiLU activation. Four layers of GATv2 [10] graph attention are then applied. GATv2 corrects the static attention problem of the original GAT formulation [11] by computing attention scores as a function of the concatenated, linearly transformed source and target node features, making attention coefficients strictly dependent on both endpoints of each edge. The first three layers use 8 attention heads with combined output (8d dimensions), maintaining dimension through residual connections. The final layer uses 4 heads with averaged output, projecting to d=64 dimensions. Edge features are injected at each layer through a learned edge projection MLP. Formally, the attention coefficient for edge (i,j) at layer l is:

alpha_ij(l) = exp( a^T * LeakyReLU( W * [h_i(l), h_j(l), e_ij] ) ) / sum over k in N(i) of exp( a^T * LeakyReLU( W * [h_i(l), h_k(l), e_ik] ) )

where W and a are learned parameters shared across all edges at layer l, and N(i) denotes the neighborhood of node i.

Transformer Encoder. GNN embeddings are enriched with global context through a six-layer pre-norm Transformer encoder [12]. Pre-norm architecture provides improved training stability at genomic data scales. Sinusoidal positional encodings indexed by normalized bin position encode chromosomal order. A learned species embedding vector is added per bin, enabling the model to represent species-specific structural patterns. Each layer applies multi-head self-attention (8 heads) followed by a position-wise feedforward network (inner dimension 512) with GELU activation and dropout (p = 0.1). The Transformer operates over all M bins simultaneously, allowing each bin's representation to be influenced by every other bin regardless of Hi-C contact status, which captures long-range structural dependencies that the local GNN cannot reach. The combined encoder contains 2,553,548 trainable parameters.

  1. Stage 4: 3D Reconstruction: Three-dimensional coordinates are recovered through two parallel pathways whose outputs are fused.

Distance Prediction Pathway. A shared MLP predicts a pairwise distance matrix D_hat of shape M by M from bin embedding pairs. For each pair of bins i and j, a feature vector is constructed by concatenating four components: the embedding of bin i (z_i), the embedding of bin j (z_j), their element-wise difference (z_i minus z_j), and their element-wise product (z_i times z_j), yielding a vector of dimension 4 times d = 256. This vector is passed through a four-layer MLP with Softplus output activation, which enforces non-negativity of all predicted distances. Symmetry is enforced as:

D_hat = (D_hat + D_hat^T) / 2

and the diagonal is set to zero, encoding that every bin has zero distance from itself.

Coordinate Decoder Pathway. A five-layer MLP decoder maps each bin embedding directly to a point in 3D space through a progressive upsampling sequence (64 to 128 to 256 to 256 to 128 to 3 dimensions) with BatchNorm and GELU activations, and a Tanh output that constrains coordinates to a unit cube.

Fusion via Distance Geometry. The predicted distance matrix D_hat is converted to 3D coordinates through Classical Multidimensional Scaling (cMDS). cMDS recovers coordinates via eigendecomposition of the double-centered Gram matrix B, computed as:

B = -0.5 * J * D_hat_squared * J

where D_hat_squared is the element-wise square of D_hat, and J = I - (1/M) * 11^T is the centering matrix with I the identity and 1 a column vector of ones. The top-3 eigenvectors of B, scaled by the square root of their corresponding eigenvalues, give the initial 3D coordinate matrix.

These coordinates are then refined by stress-minimization MDS [13], which minimizes the normalized stress:

sigma = sqrt( sum over i < j of (||x_i - x_j|| - D_hat_ij)^2 / sum over i < j of D_hat_ij^2 )

via Adam optimization over 100 iterations, where x_i and x_j are the current 3D positions of bins i and j.

MDS coordinates are aligned to the decoder coordinate frame through Procrustes analysis [14]. Given source coordinates X_MDS and target coordinates X_dec, both centered, the optimal rotation R* is found by computing the SVD of the cross-covariance matrix H = X_MDS^T * X_dec, giving H = U * S * V^T, and setting:

R* = V * diag(1, 1, det(V * U^T)) * U^T

This corrects for reflections as well as rotations. Final coordinates are a weighted average of decoder and Procrustes-aligned MDS outputs (weight 0.5 each), then passed through a learned coordinate refinement layer that iteratively adjusts positions to reduce distance residuals using learned per-iteration step sizes.

  1. Stage 5: Confidence and Uncertainty Scoring: Each predicted bin position is given a confidence score c_i between 0 and 1 by a learned scoring head that combines three signals: a distance consistency score measuring agreement between predicted pairwise distances and distances implied by the final 3D coordinates across the top-10 nearest neighbors of each bin; a normalized graph degree signal reflecting how many Hi-C contacts support each bin's structural placement; and the bin's latent embedding processed through a three-layer MLP with sigmoid output activation.

The distance consistency score for bin i is computed by identifying its top-k nearest neighbors by predicted distance, computing the absolute difference between predicted distance D_hat_ij and implied distance ||x_i - x_j|| for each neighbor j, averaging these differences, and mapping the result to a consistency value in [0, 1] by normalizing against the maximum discrepancy across all bins:

consistency_i = 1 - (mean over j in top-k neighbors of |D_hat_ij - ||x_i - x_j|||) / max_i(same quantity)

Structural domains are detected using an insulation-score approach [15]: for each bin, the average predicted distance to bins within a sliding window on each side is computed, and local maxima in this signal mark domain boundaries, analogous to published TAD boundary callers. Per-region confidence is aggregated as the mean and minimum bin confidence within each detected domain.

Coordinate uncertainty is estimated through two complementary mechanisms. A learned variance head predicts per-bin, per-axis log-variance v_ik from bin embeddings, converted to standard deviation as:

sigma_ik = exp(0.5 * v_ik)

Monte Carlo dropout [16] runs T = 10 stochastic forward passes through the reconstruction model with dropout active, and the empirical standard deviation across passes gives epistemic uncertainty sigma_ik_MC per bin per axis. Combined uncertainty per axis is:

sigma_ik_combined = sqrt( sigma_ik^2 + sigma_ik_MC^2 )

and the scalar uncertainty radius per bin is:

r_i = sqrt( sigma_i1_combined^2 + sigma_i2_combined^2 + sigma_i3_combined^2 )

interpretable as the radius of a confidence ellipsoid around each predicted 3D position.

  1. Stage 6: Explainability: At the end of it all, GenoFold delivers explainability through three mechanisms, which are each grounded in the model’s internal computations rather than post-hoc approximations.

        Integrated Gradients. Feature attribution scores are computed via Integrated Gradients (IG) [17], which attributes the model output to input features by accumulating gradients along a linear interpolation path from a zero baseline x_bar to the actual input x, approximated via a 50-step Riemann sum. The attribution score for input feature k is:

IG_k(x) = (x_k - x_bar_k) * (1/n) * sum over i from 1 to n of dF(x_bar + (i/n) * (x - x_bar)) / dx_k

where F is the GNN encoder output norm, n = 50 is the number of interpolation steps, and x_bar = 0 is the baseline. Attribution scores are aggregated by summing absolute values within four named feature groups — NGS coverage, k-mer sequence features, genomic metadata, and bin position — to produce interpretable group-level importance values revealing which modality most influenced the predicted structure.

Attention Weight Extraction. GAT attention coefficients alpha_ij are extracted from the first GATv2 layer, revealing which Hi-C contacts the model weighted most heavily during neighborhood aggregation across all 8 attention heads. Transformer self-attention weights are extracted from the final encoder layer and averaged across heads to produce an M by M attention matrix A, where A_ij represents the degree to which bin i attends to bin j when building its global structural representation. This matrix is directly interpretable as a learned, data-driven measure of functional coupling between every pair of genomic bins.

Influential Loci Detection. Bins are ranked by total incoming Transformer attention — the column sum of the attention matrix A — which measures how much aggregate attention a bin receives from all other bins. The influence score for bin j is:

influence_j = (sum over i of A_ij) / max_j(sum over i of A_ij)

normalized to [0, 1]. High-scoring bins represent candidate structural hubs, analogous to CTCF anchor sites or compartment cores, and are reported as the top-10 influential loci. These loci are surfaced in the GenoFold web interface as an interactive genomic track alongside the 3D structure viewer.

Experimental Setup:

  1. Dataset: We evaluated GenoFold on the GM12878 human lymphoblastoid cell line in-situ Hi-C dataset produced by Rao et al. [3], which publicly available through the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus under accession GSE6325. This dataset represents one of the most deeply sequenced Hi-C experiments in the literature, generated using MboI restriction enzyme digestion on in-situ ligation preparations, and is widely adopted as a standard benchmark for 3D genome reconstruction methods. NGS coverage is derived from the corresponding GM12878 DNase-seq signal track (ENCODE accession wgEncodeUwDnaseGm12878RawRep1) downloaded as a bigWig file, providing per-bin chromatin accessibility measurements that serve as the NGS modality input. Real DNA sequences and per-bin GC content are computed directly from the hg19 reference genome (UCSC, chr22.fa), ensuring that sequence-derived features reflect actual nucleotide composition rather than placeholder values. Species-level metadata uses humans as the species label with placeholder mappability values of 0.9, as a genome-wide mappability track was not included in this evaluation.
  2. Region and Resolution: All experiments are conducted on ‘chromosome 22’ at 100 kb resolution. The first 200 bins are selected for evaluation, a region that includes centromeric and pericentromeric sequence known to exhibit high sparsity. After bins are filtered with a zero-fraction threshold of 0.97, 39 bins are retained as valid nodes, producing 1,482 Hi-C contact edges in the final graph.
  3. Implementation: GenoFold is implemented in Python 3.12 using PyTorch 2.0 and PyTorch Geometrics for graph neural network operations. The full pipeline runs in a single invocation with no intervention between each stage. All experiments are conducted on CPU hardware (Apple M-series), with no GPU acceleration, confirming the architecture is accessible without highly advanced computers.
  4. Comparison with Others Tools: GenoFold is positioned against three established models: 3DMax [4], HiC-GNN [5], and FLAMINGO [6]. A separate training study is required to conduct direct quantitative benchmarking as GenoFold’s weights are not trained against real structures. This paper evaluates GenoFold’s effectiveness, while providing a comparison of its design and capabilities.

Results and Discussion: The GenoFold pipeline was executed end-to-end on the GM12878 Hi-C data through the web interface, successfully completing all six stages and producing a fully populated results dashboard. Of 200 candidate bins on chromosome 22, 39 survived filtering, producing 1,482 Hi-C contact edges with a mean confidence score of 0.504 and a mean uncertainty radius of 1.638. Six structural domains were detected across the 39-bin region, each assigned a per-region confidence score between 50% and 51%, reflecting the expected near-uniform distribution of an untrained model operating on a sparse centromeric region. The explainability stage produced four interactive outputs: a feature group importance bar chart showing k-mer sequence features as the dominant contributor to the GNN encoder's output, followed by positional encoding, NGS coverage, and genomic metadata; a Transformer self-attention heatmap displaying a structured checkerboard pattern of alternating high and low attention weights across the 39 by 39 bin matrix, consistent with the block-diagonal attention behavior expected from a genomic region with domain-like contact structure; an influential loci track identifying bins 36, 37, 4, 38, 8, 35, 34, 9, 29, and 33 as the top-10 structurally attended positions along the chromosome; and an interactive rotatable 3D structure viewer colored by per-bin confidence, providing a visual representation of the predicted chromatin fold with uncertainty encoded in the color gradient from low to high confidence.

Limitations and Future Work: The most significant limitation of GenoFold in its current form is the lack of supervised training against experimentally validated 3D genome structures. Model weights are randomly initialized in this work, and architecture validation is performed on internal consistency metrics rather than reliable coordinates. The current evaluation is further limited to a single chromosome, cell line, and resolution, so extension is necessary to establish generalization capability and validate the species embedding layer. Finally, deployment as a publicly accessible web application would make GenoFold available to the general public, particularly the broader genomic community, without the need for local installation of the full software stack.

Conclusion: GenoFold’s core contributions, which include a multi-modal graph representation, a GATv2 and Transformer encoder, a dual-pathway 3D reconstruction module, and a confidence and explainability layer, all address the single-modality dependence, interpretability gaps, and pipeline fragmentation that define existing approaches. Applied to real GM12878 in-situ Hi-C data, GenoFold successfully completes all six stages on chromosome 22, producing internally consistent 3D coordinates, confidence scores, structural domain annotations, and interactive explainability figures within a single pipeline invocation. Although there may be certain limitations due to the current model’s narrow scope, GenoFold is positioned to establish a new standard for multi-modal 3D genome reconstruction and aims to create a genuine impact in healthcare. The full implementation is publicly available at: https://github.com/synthica-org/GenoFold [19].
